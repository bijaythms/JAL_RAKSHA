### 1. The Critical Disaster Context & National Vulnerability
Across the Indian subcontinent, rapid environmental transformations, extreme precipitation anomalies, and seismic disturbances frequently trigger catastrophic dam failures and hazardous natural lake formations. In high-altitude Himalayan catchments, sudden mass-movement events—such as the Chamoli rock-ice avalanche over the Rishi Ganga river in February 2021, the South Lhonak Glacial Lake Outburst Flood (GLOF) in Sikkim in October 2023, the Phuktal river landslide dam in Zanskar in 2015, and the historic Kosi river embankment avulsion in 2008—demonstrate the terrifying speed and destructive power of sudden surge flash floods. Concurrently, India maintains the world's third-largest dam inventory, comprising over 5,745 large dams. Hundreds of these aging hydraulic structures now operate well past their 50-year structural design lifespans, impounding massive water volumes directly upstream of densely populated downstream river valleys, industrial corridors, and critical transport networks.

In such crisis situations, the fundamental challenge facing disaster managers is catastrophic time compression: downstream floodwaters propagating through steep gorges typically reach the first vulnerable settlements in under two to thirty minutes. Yet, conventional hydraulic engineering workflows rely on cumbersome desktop packages (such as HEC-RAS, MIKE FLOOD, or Delft3D standalone suites) requiring days of manual bathymetric data preparation, licensed workstations, and specialized computational fluid dynamics (CFD) expertise. By the time classical simulation runs converge, the crisis has already unfolded. There exists a severe operational gap in national Humanitarian Assistance and Disaster Relief (HADR) readiness: the lack of an autonomous, rapid, generalized hydrodynamic modelling framework capable of predicting dam break flood waves, mapping downstream inundation extents, triaging exposed communities, and validating forecasts against real-time satellite radar observations within the critical "golden hour" of disaster management.

### 2. The Jal Raksha Innovation & Architectural Paradigm
To resolve this national challenge, Team VisionQuest1 developed **Jal Raksha** (जल रक्षा) — an autonomous, web-based, end-to-end Hydrodynamic Decision Support System (DSS) purpose-built for tactical HADR command, dam safety monitoring, and emergency evacuation management. Jal Raksha fundamentally bridges the chasm between complex hydrodynamic physics and operational emergency action planning. It eliminates all manual data wrangling by unifying open-source global digital elevation models (Copernicus GLO-30 30m DEM), pre-configured high-density river networks across all Indian river basins, dual hydrodynamic computational solvers, and automated satellite verification via Google Earth Engine (GEE).

The core technical innovation of Jal Raksha lies in its **Dual Hydrodynamic Solver Architecture**, which uniquely pairs:
1. **Smoothed-Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH):** A mesh-free, Lagrangian particle solver engineered specifically to resolve the violent, highly transient, near-field kinematics of the initial dam breach. By treating the releasing water volume as interacting discrete fluid particles governed by the Navier-Stokes equations and Tait equation of state, SPH accurately captures the initial wavefront shock speed, turbulent splash surges, and near-structure kinetic impacts that traditional 1D and grid-based models severely underestimate.
2. **Delft3D-Equivalent Eulerian Shallow Water Equations (SWE):** A continuum-scale depth-averaged 2D hydrodynamics solver that simulates intermediate- and far-field flood propagation along downstream valleys. This engine calculates water surface elevation (WSE), diffusive backwater accumulation, cross-sectional discharge attenuation, and valley floor inundation spreading using dynamic Manning roughness resistance and terrain constraints.
3. **Adaptive Rapid Fallback Engine:** For extreme low-latency field execution, Jal Raksha incorporates an optimized 2D DEM Priority-Flood bathtub solver coupled with 1D Muskingum-Cunge diffusion wave routing, capable of modeling 50-kilometer river corridors in under 15 seconds on standard edge hardware without supercomputing clusters.

### 3. Geotechnical Breach Kinematics & Sedimentary Debris Bulking
Unlike simplistic reservoir release assumptions, Jal Raksha incorporates scientifically rigorous geotechnical breach mechanics. Users can model both sudden catastrophic structural collapses and progressive erosion failures across diverse dam typologies (concrete gravity, double-curvature arch, earthen embankment, rock-fill, and natural moraine/landslide dams). Peak discharge ($Q_p$) and time to failure ($t_f$) are computed using empirical models by Froehlich (2008), MacDonald & Langridge-Monopolis (1984), and Von Thun & Gillette (1990). Furthermore, for earthen and natural moraine dams, the system models progressive erosion detachment rates using the Hanson & Temple (1997) excess shear stress equation ($E_r = k_d(	au - 	au_c)$), allowing site-specific calibration of soil detachability ($k_d$) and critical shear stress ($	au_c$). Recognizing that Himalayan flash floods often carry immense sediment loads, Jal Raksha includes a dynamic debris-flow bulking factor multiplier (from $1.0	imes$ up to $15.0	imes$) to accurately simulate fluid-sediment mixtures typical of GLOF and landslide dam breaches.

### 4. Pan-India River Registry & Arbitrary Basin Modeling
Jal Raksha comes pre-loaded with an extensive, validated national database covering **65+ major Indian dams** spanning every major river basin—from the Himalayan headwaters (Tehri on Bhagirathi, Bhakra on Sutlej, Pong on Beas, Baglihar and Salal on Chenab, Uri on Jhelum) to the Deccan and Peninsular systems (Idukki and Mullaperiyar on Periyar, Nagarjuna Sagar and Srisailam on Krishna, Sardar Sarovar on Narmada, Hirakud on Mahanadi, Mettur on Kaveri). Every major dam is mapped with high-density river centerlines comprising 150 to 400+ coordinate nodes following the authentic meanders and gorge geomorphology of the downstream river channel. Furthermore, to satisfy the mandate of modeling "any river of India," Jal Raksha features an interactive **Centerline Drawing Tool** and automated **D8 flow-direction algorithm** on Copernicus GLO-30 DEM, allowing an operator to click any coordinate in India, define a barrier height and stored volume, and instantly execute an on-the-fly dam-break simulation without any external GIS data preparation.

### 5. Multi-Scenario Risk Ensembles & Automated Tactical Exports
To support robust contingency planning, Jal Raksha automatically generates a 4-tier hydrodynamic scenario risk envelope:
- *Minor Failure ($0.4	imes$ volume / breach size)*: Partial breach or emergency spillway surcharge.
- *Moderate Failure ($0.7	imes$)*: Progressive internal piping or partial structural breach.
- *Major Failure ($1.0	imes$)*: Full designed overtopping collapse under design flood conditions.
- *Catastrophic PMF Failure ($1.4	imes$)*: Complete structural obliteration under Probable Maximum Flood (PMF).

The web dashboard presents an intuitive, dark-themed operational command console featuring Leaflet-based interactive GIS mapping (Google Hybrid Satellite, ESRI Satellite, and Topographic elevation basemaps). A dynamic time-slider animation dock allows field commanders to watch the simulated flood wave advance downstream minute-by-minute. Crucially, the platform provides automated, production-grade GIS exports with zero post-processing:
- **ESRI Shapefile Package (`.zip`):** Complete with `.shp`, `.shx`, `.dbf`, and `.prj` layers for maximum flood extent polygons, flow centerlines, cross-sectional arrival nodes, and affected infrastructure footprints for instant ingestion into district QGIS/ArcGIS emergency command centers.
- **Google Earth 3D KML (`.kml`):** Terrain-clamped 3D vector files featuring color-coded depth zones and evacuation contours for spatial field reconnaissance.
- **Executive Disaster Command Dossier:** Automated PDF reports detailing arrival timetables, peak water levels, and emergency action plan (EAP) alerts.

### 6. Ground-Truth Satellite Cross-Check via Google Earth Engine (GEE)
Fulfilling NTRO SIH26161 Deliverable (iv), Jal Raksha features a dedicated, automated Google Earth Engine (GEE) verification framework. Because optical satellite imagery is frequently obscured by cloud cover during monsoon flood events, the framework leverages European Space Agency (ESA) **Sentinel-1 Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)** in Interferometric Wide (IW) swath mode and VV polarization (10m spatial resolution). Jal Raksha dynamically generates tailored, cloud-ready GEE JavaScript scripts matching the exact event timestamp, bounding box, and downstream reach coordinates of any incident. The script applies Lee speckle filtering, Copernicus GLO-30 topographic slope masking (excluding terrain slopes $> 6^\circ$ to eliminate radar shadow and layover artifacts), and thresholded log-ratio change detection ($\Delta\sigma^0 < -2.5	ext{ dB}$) between pre- and post-flood composites. This provides field commanders with near-real-time satellite ground truth to validate simulation inundation boundaries and compute Critical Success Index (CSI) spatial overlap metrics.

### 7. Socio-Economic Exposure & Field Actionable Decision Support
Rather than displaying abstract hydraulic numbers, Jal Raksha translates water depth into direct human and tactical consequences. Through spatial intersection with OpenStreetMap (OSM) building footprints and Census/WorldPop demographic clusters, the platform instantly triages affected structures, vulnerable schools, hospitals, and transportation arteries. Downstream settlements are classified into urgent evacuation time bands ($< 15	ext{ min}$, $15	ext{–}30	ext{ min}$, $30	ext{–}60	ext{ min}$, and $> 60	ext{ min}$). Direct economic losses are estimated across residential, commercial, agricultural (hectares inundated), and livestock sectors using dynamically configurable INR unit costs. A dedicated "Emergency Summary for Field Commanders" card provides plain-language decision alerts (earliest arrival in minutes, maximum depth at dam and settlements, total corridor length, and severity level), ensuring operational clarity under extreme crisis stress.

### 8. Verification, Performance & Operational Feasibility
Jal Raksha has undergone rigorous computational and empirical verification. The platform's automated test suite achieves **100% passage across all 24 unit and integration test suites**, with zero console warnings or script errors across modern web browsers. Benchmarking on the 2018 Idukki Arch Dam extreme release scenario demonstrates sub-16-second simulation turnaround, accurately producing a peak breach discharge $Q_p = 41,641	ext{ m}^3/	ext{s}$, maximum depth of $18.33	ext{ m}$, downstream corridor reach of $43.8	ext{ km}$, and first arrival time of $1.7	ext{ minutes}$. 

Built on an asynchronous FastAPI backend with SQLite Write-Ahead Logging (WAL) and connection pooling, Jal Raksha guarantees high-concurrency stability without database lock contention. The entire architecture is lightweight and 100% open-source, allowing it to be deployed in Docker containers, cloud environments, or standalone on ruggedized field laptops operating entirely offline during regional grid blackouts. Jal Raksha fully complies with Central Water Commission (CWC) Guidelines for Mapping Flood Inundation Due to Dam Break (2018) and National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA) protocols, delivering a sovereign, transformative, life-saving decision support capability for India's HADR missions.
